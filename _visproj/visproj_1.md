Emergency managers, engineers, public health officials and risk communicators rely on aftershock forecasts for critical post-earthquake decisions. I lead a multi-agency effort across the U.S., Mexico, El Salvador and New Zealand to develop new maps, graphics and interactive software for communicating aftershock forecasts to meet the varied needs of these diverse user groups. We [elicited the needs for aftershock forecast products](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212420925002742) from 12 professions in three countries, using a [novel method for stakeholder engagement](https://osf.io/download/5qam4/). We then developed 100+ [new maps and graphics for aftershock forecasts](https://pubs.geoscienceworld.org/ssa/bssa/article/doi/10.1785/0120260137/799785/Tailor-the-Product-for-Its-Use-Aftershock-Forecast) based on these needs, systematizing our approach into a [new technique for user-centered design](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/full/10.1061/NHREFO.NHENG-2894). We further built interactive software that allows users to design their own aftershock forecast maps based on their specific requirements. These product innovations have already supported urban search and rescue (among other uses) following recent earthquakes in Turkey, Morocco, and El Salvador.
